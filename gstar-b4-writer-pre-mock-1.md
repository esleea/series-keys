# Control: G-Star Writer 4, first draft (`writer-b4-pre-mock-1.pdf`)

Hand proofread on 14 October 2026 of the first draft of G-Star Writer 4, before it goes to mock. The
owner asked for five things:
- the language and difficulty on target;
- authentic English;
- the quality of the activities and of the model texts;
- above all, sections **C** (Analyze) and **F** (Brainstorm), where students analyze the model and use
  it as a sample;
- whether the callouts on the model text are right and the goals are met.

Every page was read twice: from the text layer and from a page image (130 dpi). Every Practice item was
solved, and every C organizer was answered from its model text. Two independent review agents then
checked every finding against the page images, solved every item again, tested each redesign against
B, and read every line for misses (one took L1–4, the other L5–8 and the back pages). Their
corrections and additions are merged here. Nothing paid ran.

**The lesson contract** (owner, 30 Sep):
- **B** is the model text.
- **C** analyzes B with a graphic organizer. Its labels must be about the text, and students must think,
  not copy.
- **D** teaches words useful for the student's own G writing.
- **E** practises D.
- **F** brainstorms on the same organizer as C.
- **G** is the writing task. Its support is at most genre format items, two goal-linked openers and
  paragraph markers that match F.
- **H** is a rubric that checks the goals and the plan.

Ids are `W4D-nn`. **Must fix** means wrong or harmful as drafted: an error in the English, a wrong or
double answer, a contradiction, a callout that teaches something false, content unfit for the age, or a
breach of a rule already decided. **Should fix** means a recommendation. Proposed C/F redesigns are
marked **Redesign**. They are drafts for the writers, not final copy.

## The headline

1. **The Overview (pp. 49–50) belongs to another book.** It holds one lesson only: *Lesson 1 Narrative
   Writing … Dear Diary*, with the vocabulary *lose track of time, make it on time, oversleep, make up
   for, skip a meal*. Those are **Writer 2 Lesson 1** words in the lexical list. Writer 4 has no diary
   lesson. The Overview must be rebuilt from the eight Writer 4 lessons (W4D-01).
2. **The difficulty is above the target in most lessons.** These models read at B1, and some at B2:
   L5 (a corporate press statement: *misinformation, undeniably, preparations were being carried
   out*); L6 (*food insecurity, anaphylaxis, lactose, diarrhea, raise awareness*); L8 (*insulin,
   hormone, poor wound healing*). Level 4 is recorded as A2, so either the target is out of date or the
   texts need to come down. See the level table below. **This is the main decision for the owner.**
3. **Three lessons have content that is wrong for the age:**
   - **L7** is a revenge story: kidnapping, blowing up warehouses, bleeding, and a photo of a man with a
     pistol. Its D words are *revenge* and *kidnap*.
   - **L6** includes anaphylaxis and diarrhea.
   - **L8** has a close-up photo of decayed teeth and a diabetes paragraph.
4. **The C and F organizers mirror each other well.** Every F repeats C's boxes, and every G frame's
   markers match F. But the boxes rarely ask students to *think*:
   - they are heavily pre-filled (L2, L3, L5);
   - none has a slot for the lesson's language goal (metaphors, contrast words, alliteration, emphasis
     words, opinion phrases);
   - in L7 and L8 the model text does not contain what C asks for (L7's setting; L8's "Did You Know?"
     addressing the reader).
5. **Two model texts do not fit their F tasks.** In L5, the model clears up a rumour and apologizes, but
   F's ideas (a school fair, a new business) have nothing to clear up. In L6, the model *explains causes*
   ("there are two main causes"), but F's ideas take a *position* ("why homework is bad").
6. **SB4 grammar is threaded correctly**, lesson by lesson: future continuous (L3), *would* (L4),
   passive continuous (L5), tag questions (L6), the third conditional (L7). There is no grammar
   forward reference. Four *words* come from later books: *ambitious* (SB6), *consequence* (SB6),
   *overcome* (SB5) and *manage* (SB5).

**Model vs task (L5).** B is a company correcting a rumour and apologizing. F's ideas are a school
fair, a train delay, a book release party and a new business. Students who copy B's structure (clear
up a rumour, deny it, apologize) have nothing to deny, and C's three body points do not transfer. Use
a plain announcement that the F ideas can copy, with the same SB4 L5 grammar:

> **Announcement: Sports Day Moved to May 20**
>
> Sports Day will not take place this Friday as planned.
>
> The school track **is being repaired**, so students **can't** use it this week. Sports Day will
> **definitely** take place on Friday, May 20.
>
> Students who signed up will keep their places. Families will still **be able to** watch.
>
> It will **certainly** be hot, so please bring a hat and water.
>
> We are sorry for the change, and we hope to see you all there!

This lays out as C, F and G need: an introduction, three body points and a closing.

**Redesign C/F (L5):** Title · Introduction (what is announced) · Point 1 (the most important) · Point 2 ·

That keeps G's five markers (introduction, 1, 2, 3, closing).

**Should fix (L5):**
- **B:** `Artist Ned Scott` → `the artist Ned Scott`. `First, … Secondly,` → `First, … Second,`.
  `Refund requests are being reviewed until July 10th` → `will be accepted until July 10`.
- **Word Corner:** *outrage* is negative and not usable in G. Useful words for announcements:
  *schedule, location, reminder, sign up, notice*.
- **E-A1:** `examine something carefully` → `to examine …`.
- **Image:** the ticket stamp reads `CANCELLED` (British). H: `and / or` → `and/or`.
