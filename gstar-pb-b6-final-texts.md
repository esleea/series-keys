## Text by text


**L1 Grammar Ex2, Maya's pancake machine.** P1 sets up and P2 gives the crisis cleanly. P3 is Maya stopping the blame. P4 raises the stakes. P5 and P6 end on Maya sending Leo to rest. Flow is good and the two targets arise naturally. Faults: PB6F-G02 (Leo's tense), PB6F-T06. Level fine.

**L1 Reading Ex1, the applications (personal narrative).** P1 sets the task. P2–P3 are one file each, a clean pattern. P4–P6 are the Wilson flashback: strong and the only real story, but P5 starts with no signal that it steps back in time (`Last spring, I had been working …` would help). P7 is the decision, P8 closes. The arithmetic breaks the frame (PB6F-T01), and the close `could not underestimate` is a weak last note. Register mostly right. Faults: PB6F-G14, PB6F-02, PB6F-65, PB6F-67, PB6F-68, PB6F-69.

**L1 Reading Ex2, Gigi (personal narrative).** P1 is a good portrait. P2 gives the backstory; `While Gigi was not neglected …, her story still made me a little upset` is a soft link. P3 is the vet. P4 is the longest paragraph and reads as a list. P5 is the failed visitors and Elena. P6 is the decision. P7 is a warm, earned ending and the best writing in L1. The weak link is P5 to P6: requirements set up in P4 are never paid off (PB6F-T09). P3's `While we welcomed and loved Gigi, I made it my mission` is an illogical `While` (use `We welcomed and loved Gigi, but I made it my mission`). Faults: PB6F-04, PB6F-03, PB6F-71, PB6F-72, PB6F-G15, PB6F-G16, PB6F-73, PB6F-T08, PB6F-T09, PB6F-74.

**L1 Listening scripts.** Ex1's five monologues are clear and distinct. Ex2 is a natural enough counselor talk. Ex3's four dialogues are short and sound. Faults in the audio table.

**L2 Grammar Ex1, turtle dialogue.** Real information in natural turns, and all blanks solve. Faults: PB6F-T10, PB6F-T11.

**L2 Reading Ex1, the bystander effect (explainer).** P1 is an excellent hook. P2 defines and gives the case. P3–P5 are three reasons in a clean frame, opened badly (PB6F-T12). P6 sits awkwardly: it says why learning matters before the two tips in P7–P8, and would read better just before the close. P9 is a good ending with a question. Facts are the problem (PB6F-08). `clueless` is too casual beside the rest.

**L2 Reading Ex2, OSHA (brochure).** P1 hook, P2 definition, P3 history, P4 what it does, P5 prevention, P6 worked case, P7 rights, P8 close. Well shaped for its genre, but it is long for a brochure and repeats `precautions` five times and `safety` throughout. It models `it is + adj + for` heavily, which is the point. Faults: PB6F-10, PB6F-09, PB6F-84, PB6F-85, PB6F-86, PB6F-87. Facts: agency opened 1971, correct.

**L2 Listening scripts.** Ex1's four monologues are each a small story and work well. Ex2 is the best fit to target (three `of` uses). Ex3 has a logic fault (PB6F-T50, PB6F-T51).
