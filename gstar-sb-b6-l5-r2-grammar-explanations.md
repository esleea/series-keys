# G-Star SB6 L5–Review 2: are the grammar explanations clear to a student?

This is the third companion to `gstar-sb-b6-l5-r2.md`. The first two are that file (sentence errors)
and `gstar-sb-b6-l5-r2-texts.md` (the texts as writing).

On 14 October 2026 the owner asked two things:
- whether anyone had checked the grammar section's **descriptions** ("to make assumptions …", "to
  express a change …") and **Grammar Notes** for *simple, clear explanation*, not only for errors;
- whether every Usage and Reading **models the lesson's grammar** (the second part of this file).

Nobody had checked either. The proofread fixed errors in the grammar boxes (SB6B-01, 34, 35, 63, 71
and 72; R07 and R20), but it never asked whether an 11–12-year-old at B1 could learn the point from
the box.

This file is based on the page images (printed pp. 36, 44, 52 and 60). A review agent checked every
quote and every rewrite for accuracy. It corrected five rewrites:
- one was a comma splice;
- one gave an inversion rule with no trigger;
- one contradicted its own example;
- one stated an -ing rule that is false for one-off past actions;
- one left out the negative form.

Its corrections are merged below.

**The standard applied.** A student-facing grammar explanation should:
- **Say what the structure is for in everyday words** a B1 learner already knows. No terms the
  box has not taught, such as *assumption*, *source*, *clause*, *phrase* or *emphasis*. (*be verb*
  is the usual local classroom label, so it is non-standard rather than wrong.)
- **Give the form as a pattern** the student can copy.
- **State the one rule that prevents the usual mistake**, with a wrong/right pair if needed.
- **Pair each example with its meaning** when the meaning is the point. L6's `(I prefer a bigger
  house …)` already does this well.
- **Fit the box.** Most rewrites are the same length as the print. Four of them add one line: L5
  Band 1, the L6 subjunctive form, the L7 inversion and L8 Band 2.

These are suggestions for the writers. The final wording is the owner's decision.

## Lesson 5

| Box | As printed | Why it is hard for a student | Plain rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Band 1 | Reporting Verbs + Passive Voice: `to report an information` | Explains the term with the term; an error too (SB6B-01); never says **why** you would use it | `to tell what people say or believe, without naming them` |
| Band 2 | Could / Must / Might + Have: `to make assumptions about something that already happened` | *assumption* is above B1 and abstract; the idea is a **guess about the past** and how sure you are; the heading never says **past participle** | `to guess what happened, and show how sure you are` |
| Note, why | `we aren't sure whether the information is true` / `we want to put some distance between us and the information (we are not the source of the information)` | Bullet 1 is clear. Bullet 2 is a metaphor plus *source* (B2), and its two lines repeat each other | Keep bullet 1. Bullet 2: `it is what other people say, not what we know ourselves. (News reports use it a lot.)` |
| Note, form | `It + be verb + reporting verb + that` / `Subject + be verb + reporting verb + …` | It does not say the reporting verb must be the **past participle** (`It is say that` is the likely error) | `It + is/was + said/believed + that …` → `It is said that he is rich.` / `He + is/was + said/believed + to …` → `He is said to be rich.` |
| Note, the three to-forms | `to-infinitive for now or future` / `to + be + verb-ing for action in progress` / `to + have + past participle for past actions` | `for now or future` is **wrong** for its own example (`She is known to work …` is a habit). *action in progress* is teacher language | `to + verb` → `She is known to work there. | Note, modals | `Must have is used for things that we are certain about.` / `Might / may / could have is for things that we are less certain about.` / `Couldn't have is for things we are certain are not true.` | Accurate but abstract. It never says these are **guesses from clues** (which is why *must have* is not "certain"), and it never states the form | `could / must / might + have + past participle` / `Her lights are off.` → `She must have gone to bed. (I'm almost sure.)` / `She might / may / could have gone out. (Maybe.)` / `She couldn't have gone out. Her car is still here. (I'm sure she didn't.)` |
| Verb list | `consider`, `rumor` listed with the rest | `It is considered that …` is rare; *rumor* works only in the passive (`is rumored to`) | Mark them: `consider` → `He is considered to be …`; `rumor` → `It is rumored that … / He is rumored to …` |

## Lesson 6

| Box | As printed | Why it is hard | Plain rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Band 1 | Double Comparatives: `to express a change that happens because something else increases or decreases` | 13 words, two abstract verbs, the idea at the end | `to show that as one thing goes up or down, another thing does too` |
| Band 2 | Present Subjunctive: `to show wishes, requests, demands, or suggestions` | *wishes* is **misleading**. *I wish* takes the past (`I wish I had …`, taught in SB5), so students write `I wish that he go` | `to say what someone wants to happen: suggest / insist / demand + that + base verb` |
| Note, double comparatives | `Double comparatives show cause and effect. The first half of the sentence usually shows the cause, while the second half shows the effect.` | *cause and effect* plus *usually* leaves the student unsure, and the form is never a pattern | `The + -er/more …, the + -er/more …` → `The more you practice, the better you get.` (the first part changes → the second part changes) |
| Note, shorter forms | `The bigger the mistake, the harder to fix.`; `the …, the better` for preferences | Both clipped forms are correct English (*the sooner the better*), but students do not see the full form | Add as an option, not a rule: `You can also say: The bigger the mistake, the harder it is to fix.` |
| Note, subjunctive form | `The subjunctive verb remains as the base verb form.` | Stiff, and it does not say **what is special**: no *-s* for he/she, *be* for everyone, no past form, and the negative is *not* + verb. With I/you/we it looks normal, so students never notice | `After suggest / insist / demand that, use the base verb for everyone, even in the past: He suggested that she **talk** to Mrs. Woods. (not talks / talked) They requested that we **be** quiet. (not were) Negative: that he **not talk**. (not doesn't talk)` |
| Note, clauses | `Subjunctive sentences often have two clauses or more.` + `Clause 1 / Clause 2` on `I suggest we stay home.` | *clause* is untaught; the *we* example hides the subjunctive; *that* disappears without a note | `You can leave out that: I suggest (that) he stay home. (Not: I suggest him to stay home.)` (a common error from Chinese word order) |
| Word list | `Words Often Used in Subjunctive Sentences for Requests: insist advise propose request demand ask` | It does not say *that* + base verb follows; *ask that* and *advise that* are formal | Head it `+ that + base verb:`; mark `ask` and `advise` as formal |

## Lesson 7

| Box | As printed | Why it is hard | Plain rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Band 1 | Perfect Participle Phrases: `to describe a completed action or show cause and effect` | Two jobs, both abstract | `to show one action finished before another (and often caused it)` |
| Band 2 | Inverted Sentence: `to put emphasis, usually in formal situations` | *emphasis* and *inverted* are not explained, and the student is not told what moves or **when** | `for strong, formal sentences: Never / Rarely / Only … first, then helping verb + subject` |
| Note, participle | `Perfect participle phrases describe an action that was completed before the main sentence.` | *before the main sentence* means "before the other action". The **same-subject rule** is missing, and the book's own texts break it (SB6B-07, 23) | `Having studied hard, **she** got a high score. ✔ (she studied, she scored)` / `Having studied hard, the test was easy. ✘ (the test didn't study!)` The subject must be the same person or thing. | Note, how to invert | *(not stated: only shown in the tables)* | The key step is missing: **add do/does/did when there is no helping verb.** Students write `Never I have felt` and `Only then I watched` | `Never + have / is / can + subject: Never have I felt so tired.` / `No helping verb? | Note, only-phrases | `The phrase after only when, only if, only after, and only by doesn't get inverted, the second part of the sentence is.` | One long sentence with a comma splice (R20) that points the student at the wrong half | `After only when / if / after / by + words, the first part stays normal. ## Lesson 8

| Box | As printed | Why it is hard | Plain rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Band 1 | Reduced Relative Clauses: `to use relative clauses without the relative pronouns` | Defines the term by itself, with two untaught terms. The band's own example **changes** the verb (`who study → studying`) instead of only dropping words | `to say it in fewer words: leave out who / which / that, and drop or change the verb after it` |
| Band 2 | Wh-words / How + -ever: `to express "it doesn't matter / no matter …"` | Clear enough, but it does not warn that this *however* is not *however* = *but* | Keep the band. Add: `However you try = No matter how you try. (However + comma = but.)` |
| Note, list | Six labels: `Remove the relative pronoun`, `present participle`, `past participle`, `prepositional phrase`, `to-infinitive phrases, usually after the first/second, the only, the last, or superlatives`, `adjective phrases` | Six grammar terms, four untaught. The student needs **what to do**. The key limit is missing: *who / that* alone can go only when it is the object (`the book I love`) | Group by what you do: **① Drop who/which/that when another subject follows:** `the book (that) I love`. **② Drop who/which/that + is/are/was:** `the boy (who was) talking to me`, `the house (that was) built in 1990`, `the books (that are) on the shelf`, `the man (who was) guilty of vandalism` (keep the commas if the clause had them). **③ Active verb → -ing, for what people are doing or usually do:** `the people (who live) → living there` (not for one finished past action: `the boy who broke it`). **④ After the first / the last / the only → to + verb:** `the first person to finish`. |
| Other -ever words | `whenever, whichever, whoever, whatever, wherever` (list only) | No meanings, so *whichever* and *whatever* look the same | `whenever = any time · wherever = anywhere · whoever = anyone · whatever = anything · whichever = any one of a group` |

## What this means for the book

- **Every band line except L8 Band 2 relies on a word the student has not been taught, or explains the
  term with itself.** The rewrites use only everyday words.
- **Three notes leave out the one rule that would prevent the book's own errors:**
  - L7's same-subject rule for perfect participles (the danglers in SB6B-07 and 23);
  - L7's *do / does / did* when inverting;
  - L8's rule that *who / that* alone can be dropped only when it is the object.
- **One description is wrong and one is misleading:** L5's `for now or future`, and L6's `wishes`.
- **The core is not affected.** These rewrites are print wording. Whether the series core's
  grammar-point descriptions should also be simplified is an owner decision, because they feed irs
  and Create content.

## Does every Usage and Reading model its lesson's grammar?

The owner also asked (14 Oct) whether each Usage and the Reading contain the lesson's grammar points.
The first proofread recorded this only in general terms. Below, every text is checked for each
point. Instances were found with a pattern search over the manuscript texts, then every one was
read by hand: false hits were removed, and misused instances are marked ✘ with their id in the main
report. **Visible** means a student can actually see the structure. For the subjunctive, that needs
a he/she/it subject or *be*, because `I suggest that you go` looks exactly like the indicative.

### Lesson 5: reporting verbs + passive; could / must / might + have

| Text | Reporting passive | Could/must/might have | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | `It's rumored to be haunted`, `are said to be heard and seen` | `must have cost` (forced: texts review) | ✔ both |
| Usage 1 | `are advertised to be` (*advertise* is not in the box's verb list) | `must have washed off` (no evidence: texts review) | ✔ both, weak |
| Usage 2 | `was said to be` | `must have overlooked`, `couldn't have been` | ✔ both |
| Usage 3 | `It is known that`, `is rumored to have been bought` | `may have stolen`, `couldn't have been`, `might have stolen` | ✔ both |
| Reading | about 10 (`is widely believed to`, `is said to`, `is reported to have been`, `claimed to have encountered`, `It was believed that`; `is described to be` ✘ SB6B-04). `claimed to have encountered` is active, so it models *to have* + past participle but is not a reporting passive | `might have been` ✘ (R34), `could not have lived`, `may have been` | ✔ both |

**Gap:** the Grammar Note teaches three to-forms, and one never appears in any L5 text. *To* +
verb and *to have* + past participle are both modelled, but **to be + -ing** (`She is known to be
working …`) is not. Add one, e.g. in the Opening: `The owner is said to be living abroad now.`

### Summary of this check

A second agent checked this section independently. It added missed instances, removed one false
count, and softened two verdicts. Those changes are merged above.

- **Every Usage and every Reading contains at least one example of each of its lesson's two grammar
  points.** None is missing a point entirely.
- **Weak spots:**
  - The **L6 Opening, Usage 1 and Reading** show no subjunctive a student can see. L6's Reading
    has none at all: both of its instances have plural subjects, and one is misused (SB6B-13).
  - The **L7 Opening**, which is the lesson's first model, has one broken inversion and one
    incomplete one.
- **Two taught sub-forms are never modelled in any text:** L5's **to be + -ing** and L8's
  **how-ever / no matter how**. L7's `seldom` and `rarely` (in the word box) are not used either.
- **A correction to the main report:** SB6B-18 and SB6B-19 are in the L7 **Opening** (p51), not
  the Reading. The page number there is right.
