# G-Star SB6 L5–Review 2: the texts as writing (authenticity, level, writing quality)

A companion to `gstar-sb-b6-l5-r2.md`. That report checked sentences: grammar, word use, keys and
facts. This file judges the **20 texts** (each lesson's Opening, Usages 1–3 and Reading) as writing.
It asks four questions of each:
- Does it read like a real text of its kind?
- Is its level right for the target?
- Does each paragraph do its job and lead into the next?
- Does the logic hold?

The owner asked for this on 14 October 2026, after the same gap in the Writer 4 review.

**Target.** The series record (dev and production, read 14 Oct) gives G-Star Level 6 a CEFR target
of **B1**, with a series age range of 5–12, so SB6 is for about 11–12. The ceiling is what G-Star
SB/PB/Writer Starter–5 and SB6 up to the same lesson teach. Words were profiled with the CEFR-J
vocabulary list (`data/sources/cefr-j/cefrj-vocabulary-profile-1.5.csv`, A1–B2) and the G-Star
taught list (`data/series/g-star-lexical-resolution.json`). Texts come from the manuscript and were
checked against the print.

**Second review.** Two review agents checked every critique against the print pages and every
repair sentence by sentence. They found that several of my repairs dropped the lesson's own
grammar or vocabulary, that one repair broke a comprehension key, and that a few points were
wrong. The rows below are corrected, and their additions are at the end. **Process rule for the
writers:** after any rewrite, re-check every comprehension item, the L8 gapped text (key 1C 2E 3G
4A 5D) and the L7 CQs, because they depend on specific sentences.

**Repair scale.** The book is at print stage, so the repairs are graded:
- **must** — a logic hole, a factual error, unfit content, or a genre that does not work;
- **should** — authenticity and register;
- **could** — polish.

## The pattern across the four lessons

1. **The target grammar is forced into registers where nobody would use it.** This is the main
   authenticity fault. The structure is correct, but no real writer of that text would choose it.

   | Lesson | Text | As printed |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | L5 | forum post | `It is known that she never respects others` |
   | L5 | livestream post | `Building it must have cost a lot of money` (irrelevant to a ghost hunt) |
   | L5 | livestream post | `Strange noises and shadowy figures are said to be heard and seen` |
   | L6 | teens' chat | `I suggest that he be more sensible next time` |
   | L6 | farewell email | `The bigger the loss, the harder to recover` |
   | L8 | text to Mom | `Jerry and Andrea, responsible for the costumes, got into a disagreement` |
   | L8 | mayor | `Thank you to the people supportive of our new garbage collection project` |
| L7 | shop flyer | `Under no circumstances could we manage to operate for so long without your support` (*under no circumstances* is for rules, not thanks) |

   Real models put the structure where its register belongs: a passive report in news, a
   subjunctive in a formal email, a reduced relative in an ad or an article.
2. **The readings are above B1; the openings and usages mostly are not.** All four readings run
   600–650 words. L5, L6 and L8 carry a B2 (and some C1) vocabulary load, much of it unglossed
   (table below). L7 is close to B1, apart from fencing terms.
3. **Three readings have no conclusion, or a conclusion that does not follow.** L5 stops after
   Mothman and never answers its title question. L6's last two sections leave its stated topic
   (social media). L8 ends on a moral its examples do not support.
4. **Several texts have logic holes a careful 12-year-old would notice.** For example:
   - L5 Usage 3: the roommate owns the apartment, so "complain to the landlord" is impossible.
   - L5 Usage 2: the money "disappeared", but it had simply not been recorded.
   - L6 Usage 1: a teammate is "the only friend I can talk to about basketball".
   - L8 Reading: James "lies" to cope, though the text has just said lying causes dissonance.

## Lesson 6

### Opening: Siberian husky advice (p43)

A good, authentic model: an advice post with a hook, bullet facts, a recommendation and a fair
closing. The double comparatives (`The less exercise …, the more bored …`) and the subjunctive
(`I suggest that you reconsider`) sit naturally in it. Two polish points:
- five of the sentences start with *Huskies*;
- the capitals are mixed (`Husky facts` vs `raising a husky`);
- the subjunctive is invisible (`I suggest that you reconsider`, `I recommend that you do` look like the indicative). Make one visible: `I recommend that every new owner learn more about their coat care …`;
- `unusual behaviors, such as howling, chewing …`: these are normal husky behaviors when bored → `destructive behaviors, such as chewing, scratching, and digging`.

### Usage 2: dialogue about Albert (p45)

The logic is clear. The register is the problem. `I suggest that he be more sensible next time`
and `Miss Lydia demanded that he stay for detention` are formal subjunctives in a chat between two
students. A teacher *gives* detention. **Repair:** `…so Miss Lydia insisted that he stay after school.` and `Someone should tell him to be more careful. Honestly, I'd suggest he apologize to Miss Lydia.` Both keep a visible subjunctive. (U3's online comment `I suggest that others not spread negativity` can carry it: a public comment is semi-formal writing.) Grade: should.

### Usage 3: book adaptation news (p46)

A believable entertainment-news model with real comments, and the best text in L6. Two points:
- In `"I believe that Know Pictures will turn the story that we know and love …"`, *the story we know
  and love* is a fan's phrase. The author would say `my story` or `this story`.
- The headline has no byline marker (`By Anna Torres`).

It also contradicts L7 (main report SB6B-60).

### Usage 1: math champion interview (p53)

Believable and warm, and the inversions (`Only after …`, `Little did we know …`) are natural in a
speech. **Word in context:** `I felt very discouraged, and my nerves persisted`. He means
*nervous*; *discouraged* means losing hope after failure, and it is this lesson's own vocabulary
word, modelled wrongly. **Repair:** `I felt very nervous, and the feeling persisted throughout the competition.` (keeps *persist*). `It's my first time` can stay: `on the way here` puts him at the venue. Grade: must (the vocabulary misuse).

### Usage 2: gymnastics class ad (p53)

A real ad with clear terms and conditions, and a good model. But the `$150 deposit¹` points to a note that is not printed: add `¹ A deposit is money you pay first and get back later.`; and `A 20% fee will be charged to switch your class slot` → its own bullet: `Switching to another class time costs 20% of the program fee.` `Not only can you attend the classes,
but you'll also get …` is a correct and natural inversion. The fixes are in the main report (R15–R16).
